Security Analysis of ARCANE-Octarine
Mounir IDRASSI
Version 1.0.0, 23 September 2026

ARCANE-Octarine is a Fiat–Shamir signature scheme using learning with rounding
over a radical ring and a power-of-two modulus. This report examines the
submitted 128-, 256-, and 512-level profiles and all three implementation
families. The SM3 counter construction admits a generic chosen-message
signature-transfer attack near 2^128 classical work, despite its extended
output. A 344-bit descriptor determines the complete SM3-expanded secret
polynomial vector, bounding equivalent-key search. Binary linear algebra
solves the relaxed homogeneous SIS instances used in the 512-level estimates
on a constant fraction of random instances; a 5,120-row certificate and
separate verifier are provided. That certificate violates the actual
signature's stricter response bound and is not a forgery.

The report also derives a secret-free challenge-search forgery strategy,
distinguishes quantum query counts from gate costs, examines quantitative
proof obligations and ring geometry, and confirms that Algorithm 2 and the
implementations define different challenge maps at levels 128 and 256.
Neither supplied hash family supports 512-bit message-collision strength.
The evidence does not establish a practical forgery or recovery of an
unknown signing key. Revised claims, primitives, normative specifications,
and analysis of the actual verification relations are required.
